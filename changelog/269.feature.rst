Add `~stixpy.coordinates.fov.get_illumination_status` to check whether a source at a given location fully illuminates the detectors (``"full"``), shadows one pixel row (``"single row"``), partially or fully shadows some pixel columns (``"partial"``, the maximal field of view of the paper), or lies outside the field of view, following the geometry in Appendix C of Massa et al. (2023). For full or single-row illumination, it also returns which pixels to use (``"top+bot"``, ``"top"`` or ``"bot"``), which can be passed to `~stixpy.calibration.visibility.create_meta_pixels`. For partial illumination or locations outside the field of view, it returns `None` because no pixel selection is supported by the current visibility calculation. Scalar coordinates return scalar values, while coordinate arrays return arrays of the same shape. Locations in other frames, such as Helioprojective, are transformed to the STIX imaging frame at their ``obstime``. Add `~stixpy.visualisation.plotters.plot_fov` to plot the full sensitivity, single row and partial field of view boxes, optionally marking source locations with their status. The plot is drawn on a blank map in the STIX imaging frame by default, and source locations given in another frame are transformed to it. With ``frame`` the boxes and sources are plotted in that frame instead, for example Helioprojective with Solar Orbiter as observer, and ``draw_limb`` and ``draw_grid`` add the solar limb and heliographic grid. With ``ax`` the boxes and sources are drawn on top of an existing map plot, such as a back projection, in that map's frame and keeping its view, and extra keyword arguments style the source marker.
